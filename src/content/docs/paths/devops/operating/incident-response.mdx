## The roles (any team size)

- **Incident commander** — coordinates, doesn't code. Makes decisions.
- **Communications** — updates status page, customers, internal Slack.
- **Subject experts** — actually fixing.

In a small team, one person can wear two hats; never all three.

## The first 10 minutes

1. **Acknowledge.** Turn off the alarm, take ownership.
2. **Stabilize > investigate.** Roll back if you can. Disable the feature. Throttle traffic.
3. **Communicate.** Status page: "We're investigating issues with X."
4. **Logs and dashboards open.** Look at the timeline of recent deploys / config changes.

## The first 60 minutes

- Identify the cause enough to fix or work around.
- Update status page every 15-30 min, even with "still investigating."
- Don't theorize publicly. State facts.

## After

- Status page: incident closed.
- **Blameless postmortem** within 48 hours. What happened, what we did, what we'll change. **People-blame doesn't fix systems.**
- Action items with owners and dates.

## Tools

- **Status pages**: Statuspage, Instatus, your own.
- **PagerDuty / Opsgenie** for on-call rotation.
- **Slack channel per incident** for the running log.
